\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why does frontier width determine noise robustness?}
The answer lies in the width of the adaptation frontier at any given step.
On Aircraft, 100 variant classes share nearly identical silhouettes, decision
boundaries are extremely close, and at any training step a substantial fraction
of clean samples remain genuinely contested --- their loss is still actively
declining, yielding a strong positive progress signal.
A corrupted label never produces a sustained decline: the rank-$r$ LoRA update
cannot reconcile a mislabelled sample with consistent gradients from visually
similar correct classes, so its loss stagnates and $s_i^{\text{rel}} \to 0$.
The frontier is wide enough that velocity discriminates cleanly between
corrupted and informative samples.
On CUB-200 and Stanford Dogs classes are more visually separable: clean samples
are learned quickly, the frontier shrinks early, and noisy samples stagnate
alongside harder clean samples near class boundaries.
EWL cannot distinguish these two groups, and mislabelled examples occasionally
receive high weights, degrading generalisation.
Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism} confirms this mechanistically: on Aircraft, per-sample
(EMA, velocity) clusters for clean and noisy samples separate clearly and the
per-epoch clean/noisy weight-separation score grows positive, whereas on CUB and
Dogs the clusters overlap and the separation score stays near or below zero.

\paragraph{When to use EWL.}
EWL is appropriate when all three conditions hold: (i)~the dataset has high
inter-class similarity, sustaining a wide adaptation frontier; (ii)~label noise
is present or suspected; (iii)~the $5.9\times$ step-time overhead is acceptable.
EWL should be avoided when classes are visually separable (the frontier narrows,
signal is non-discriminative, noise may be amplified), under class imbalance
(the velocity mechanism is frequency-blind), or in single-pass/streaming
settings (the EMA never converges).
Cost-benefit notes, practical runtime diagnostics, and an extended limitations
discussion are in Appendix~\ref{app:discussion}.
