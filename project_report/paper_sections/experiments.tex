\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup.}
We use ViT-Small/16~\cite{dosovitskiy2021image} pretrained on ImageNet-21k
(21.7M params) via \texttt{timm}~\cite{wightman2021timm}.
LoRA adapters ($r{=}4$) are applied to $W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O$ in all 12 attention
blocks (307K trainable, 1.41\% of total); the classifier head is fully
fine-tuned.
We evaluate on \textbf{FGVC-Aircraft}~\cite{maji2013fgvc} (100 variants;
extreme similarity), \textbf{CUB-200-2011}~\cite{wah2011cub} (200 birds;
moderately separable), and \textbf{Stanford
Dogs}~\cite{khosla2011stanforddogs} (120 breeds).
All models train for 50 epochs with AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw}
($\eta{=}5{\times}10^{-4}$, cosine decay); EWL defaults are $\alpha{=}0.9$,
$\tau{=}1.0$, warmup $K{=}48$.
Results are mean\,$\pm$\,std over three seeds $\{11,22,33\}$.
We compare \textbf{SFT} (LoRA baseline), \textbf{EWL} (full method), and
\textbf{EWL (no proxy)} ($g\equiv 1$).
Full protocols are in Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}.

\paragraph{Clean accuracy and proxy ablation.}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports top-1 accuracy on clean data.
EWL with the proxy matches SFT within standard deviation on all three
benchmarks ($\Delta\!=\!+0.05, +0.16, -0.01$); we do not claim clean-data gains.
The decisive result is the no-proxy ablation: removing the gradient proxy
while retaining the progress signal causes a $\mathbf{-6.76\%}$ drop on
Aircraft, with smaller but consistently negative drops on CUB-200 and Dogs.
Aircraft's 100 visually similar classes have densely packed decision boundaries;
indiscriminate early reweighting, before the EMA has stabilised, amplifies
unstable gradient directions and the model cannot recover.
Training curves (Appendix~\ref{app:curves}) confirm the gap is a training
instability rather than a final-epoch artefact.

\paragraph{Noise robustness --- dataset-conditional.}
We inject uniform random label noise into the training set at rates of 10--40\%
and evaluate on the clean test set.
Table~\ref{tab:noise_results} and Figure~\ref{fig:noise_delta} show a striking
divergence.
On Aircraft, EWL improves over SFT by $+4.26\%$ to $+6.81\%$ across all noise
levels.
On CUB-200, the gap is mildly negative ($-0.9$ to $-1.6$); on Stanford Dogs it
drops to $-6.55\%$ at 40\% noise.
This divergence is the central empirical finding of this work; the mechanism
is analysed in \S\ref{sec:discussion}.
Full noise results at every level are in Appendix~\ref{app:noise}.

\paragraph{Ablations and overhead.}
We sweep LoRA rank $r \in \{2,4,8,16\}$, softmax temperature
$\tau \in \{0.5,\dots,3.0\}$, EMA decay $\alpha \in \{0.5,\dots,1.0\}$, and
class imbalance $\text{IF}\in\{1,2,5,10,20\}$ on Aircraft (all tables and
figures in Appendix~\ref{app:hparam}--\ref{app:imbalance}).
Summarising: accuracy increases monotonically with rank ($57.40$ at $r{=}2$ to
$62.08$ at $r{=}16$); $\tau{=}0.5$ over-concentrates and hurts, while
$\tau\!\geq\!1.0$ is stable within $0.2\%$; EWL provides no meaningful benefit
under class imbalance.
Step-time overhead is $5.9\times$ SFT (stable $\sim$265\,ms vs.\ $\sim$45\,ms)
with \textbf{zero} GPU memory overhead: both conditions peak at 9.46\,GB.
The cost comes from CPU-side per-sample operations (EMA update, signal,
softmax), serialised by CPU$\leftrightarrow$GPU synchronisation rather than
floating-point throughput; vectorising these onto GPU tensors is natural
future work.
