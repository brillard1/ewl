\begin{abstract}
LoRA fine-tuning treats all training examples equally, yet sample informativeness
shifts continuously: mastered samples contribute near-zero gradients while
conflicting samples never converge, leaving only a shrinking \emph{adaptation
frontier} where loss is actively declining.
We propose \textbf{EWL (Example Weighting via Learning Progress)}, which tracks
per-sample loss velocity via exponential moving averages and upweights frontier
samples using a LoRA gradient proxy --- the mean Frobenius norm of adapter
gradients --- as an adaptive temperature, with no meta-gradients, held-out
data, or inference cost.
On three fine-grained benchmarks (FGVC-Aircraft, CUB-200-2011, Stanford
Dogs), the gradient proxy is essential: ablating it causes a $-6.76\%$ drop on
Aircraft, where ungated reweighting destabilises training on narrow decision
boundaries.
EWL's noise robustness is dataset-conditional --- on Aircraft it improves over
SFT by $+4.3$--$6.8\%$ under 10--40\% label noise, while on CUB-200 and Dogs
it is harmful ($-0.9\%$ to $-6.6\%$), as the progress signal cannot discriminate
corrupted from informative samples when the adaptation frontier is narrow.
On clean data, EWL matches SFT within standard deviation, at a $5.9\times$
step-time overhead with zero additional GPU memory.
\end{abstract}
