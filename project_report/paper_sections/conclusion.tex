\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented EWL, a progress-adaptive sample weighting method for LoRA
fine-tuning that gates per-sample loss velocity with a LoRA gradient proxy ---
no meta-gradients, no held-out data, no inference cost.
Two findings stand out: the gradient proxy is essential (ablating it drops
Aircraft by $-6.76\%$), and noise-robustness gains are dataset-conditional,
improving SFT by $+4.3$--$6.8\%$ on Aircraft's wide frontier, by
$+1.9$--$2.8\%$ on CUB-200 (realised earlier in training), and matching SFT
within noise on Stanford Dogs.
EWL matches SFT on clean data and is best suited to fine-grained, noise-prone
tasks; its applicability is tied directly to adaptation frontier width.
Future work: vectorising the per-sample loop to cut the $5.9\times$ overhead,
combining velocity with class-aware reweighting, and extending the frontier
hypothesis to language models and other PEFT methods.
